09  ·  Attention Over Time

Embedding Continuous Time

A positional encoding that accepts any real $t$.

Let's introduce a new type of encoding, the sinusoidal encoding describes a position as a bank of waves. Its coordinates come in pairs, and pair $j$ turns at its own frequency $\omega_j$:

$$PE(p,\, 2j) = \sin(\omega_j\, p), \qquad PE(p,\, 2j+1) = \cos(\omega_j\, p)$$

The frequencies are fixed in advance, spread geometrically so that the first pair turns quickly and the last pair barely turns at all across the whole sequence. Now look at what that formula actually requires in order to be evaluated. Position $p$ enters only through $\sin$ and $\cos$, and a sine does not care whether the number it is handed is a whole one. Nothing here ever asked for an integer. We fed it integers because we had a list, and the moment we stop using a list we can hand it $t = 300$, or $t = 300.5$, or $t = 2650$, and it will retrieve us a value.

So hand it the timestamp. Write $\phi(t)$ for the same encoding evaluated at a real time rather than an index, so that the window beginning at minute 300 carries $\phi(300)$ and the lactate drawn at minute 2650 carries $\phi(2650)$. Every complaint in section 06 closes at once. Two observations 340 minutes apart now differ by 340 minutes' worth of turning, whatever their positions in the list, and the encoding can no longer be made to say adjacent about a pair that is not.

The shift property survives the change and improves with it, that is, $\phi(t + \Delta)$ is still $\phi(t)$ rotated by $\omega_j \Delta$ in each pair, except that $\Delta$ is now measured in minutes, so four hours earlier is one fixed rotation the model can learn once and apply anywhere in the stay.

The Learned Time Embedding

The construction has a name and a short history. A bank of learned sines alongside one linear coordinate was proposed as a general representation of time by Time2Vec. Formally,

$$\phi(t)[i] = \begin{cases} \omega_0\, t + \alpha_0, & i = 0 \\[4pt] \sin\!\big(\omega_i\, t + \alpha_i\big), & 1 \leq i < d_r \end{cases}$$

Term by term:

  • $t$ is a time, in minutes, and any real one will do. It is not an index and there is no list it has to belong to.
  • $d_r$ is the width of the embedding, so $\phi(t)$ is a vector of $d_r$ numbers, one per coordinate $i$.
  • $\omega_i$ and $\alpha_i$ are learned, a frequency and a phase per coordinate. The model discovers which timescales matter rather than inheriting them from a formula written for sentences.
  • Coordinate $i = 0$ is the linear term, the one that is not a wave. It grows with $t$ and never returns, which is what lets the embedding distinguish early from late rather than only recognising a rhythm.
